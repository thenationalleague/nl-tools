How a goal call is assembled

A goal gets a two-beat treatment. First the tease — an intro naming the venue plus a question keyed to the match situation before the goal, while the map score is still hidden. Then the reveal — a phrase keyed to what this goal means, with a timing tag sometimes appended, and (55% of the time) one extra storyline sentence chosen by weighted lottery from every candidate whose condition is true.

⚠ The bug you caught, precisely: "consolation" is the fallback bucket — any goal that leaves the scorer still behind, by any margin, at any minute. 2–0 → 2–1 in the 72nd lands here and gets "pulls one back / a consolation" — when it is firmly game on. The classification is score-only; it never looks at the clock or at how big the remaining deficit is. Suggested split: a pullback register (deficit now 1, up to roughly the 82nd — "game on!", "right back in it", "one more and it's level") with consolation reserved for a remaining deficit of 2+ or genuinely late strikes.

Goal tease — situation questions

deadlock — it was 0–0

  • — the deadlock's broken! But who's got the opener, {home} or {away}?
  • — and someone's in front! Is it {home}, or {away}?
  • — the first goal of the afternoon! But which side?
  • — we're off the mark! {home}… or {away}?
  • — someone's drawn first blood! But who?

level — level, but not 0–0

  • — and somebody's nudged in front again! {home} or {away}?
  • — the deadlock's broken once more! But by whom?
  • — someone's edged ahead! Is it {home}, or {away}?
  • — we've got a new leader! But which one?

lead1 — one goal in it

  • — but is it a second for {leader}, or an equaliser for {trailer}?
  • — have {leader} doubled up, or {trailer} pulled level?
  • — {leader} out of sight, or {trailer} right back in it?
  • — two for {leader}, or a lifeline for {trailer}?
Tease nuance: "is it a second for {leader}" assumes the leader is on exactly one goal — at 2–1 the next would be their third. "Doubled up" has the same assumption. Only strictly true when the score is 1–0.

lead2 — two or more in it

  • — are {leader} running riot, or {trailer} pulling one back?
  • — another for {leader}, or a consolation for {trailer}?
  • — {leader} piling it on, or {trailer} with a reply?
⚠ Same register issue as the reveal: "a consolation for {trailer}" in the lead2 tease fires at 2–0 in the 55th minute — where a goal back would make it a one-goal game with half an hour left. Not a consolation.

Goal reveal — by significance

The team is named once, then referred to collectively ("they"). A timing tag (see Timing tags below) is appended: always in stoppage time, in the first 5 minutes and from the 85th; otherwise a 55% coin-flip when the window offers one.

opener

  • …and it's {team}! {scorer} breaks the deadlock — they lead {scoreNum}.
  • It's {team}! {scorer} with the opener.
  • {scorer}! They're in front, {scoreNum}.

equaliser

  • …and it's {team}! {scorer} levels it up.
  • It's {team}! {scorer} with the equaliser — game on!
  • {scorer} hauls them level at {scoreNum}.

goahead

  • …and it's {team}! {scorer} puts them ahead, {scoreNum}.
  • It's {team}! {scorer} turns it around.
  • {scorer}! They lead it now, {scoreNum}.
Nuance: "turns it around" implies a comeback, but goahead only means the score was level — 0–0 → 1–0 after a missed sitter is not "turned around". (The comeback case is separately detected by the "turned this right around" storyline, which does check they were behind.)

extend

  • …and it's {team} again! {scorer} makes it {scoreNum}.
  • It's {team}! {scorer} adds {another} — they're pulling clear.
  • {scorer} with {another} — they're cruising, {scoreNum}.
Nuance: "they're cruising" on a 2–1 → 3–1 goal in the 88th is defensible; on 1–0 → 2–0 in the 15th it's early to say. {another} renders "a second / a third / a fourth", then plain "another" from the fifth.

consolation

  • …and it's {team} — {scorer} pulls one back.
  • It's {team}! {scorer} with a consolation, {scoreNum}.
  • {scorer} gets one back for them — {scoreNum}.
Your reported case — 0–2 down, 72nd minute, rendered todayIt's Aldershot Town! Brody Peart with a consolation, 2–1. Into the final quarter.
⚠ Variant 1 ("pulls one back") survives a register fix; variant 2 is the offender; variant 3 is neutral. A pullback register wants lines like: "…they're right back in this one", "— game on at {venue}!", "one more and it's all square".

og (own goal)

  • …oh no — an own goal! {scorer} turns into his own net, and {team} benefit.
  • Disaster! {scorer}'s own goal hands it to {team} — {scoreNum}.
  • Into his own net! {scorer} gifts it to {team}.
Nuance: "hands it to {team}" reads as decisive — odd when the OG merely makes it 4–1. OG reveals also skip the timing tag entirely, so a 94th-minute own-goal winner loses its "what a time" drama.

penalty (overrides the sig pools, except og)

  • …and it's {team} — {scorer} slots the penalty! {scoreNum}.
  • It's {team}! {scorer} sends the keeper the wrong way from the spot.
  • {scorer} steps up… and buries the penalty. {scoreNum}.
  • From the spot — {scorer} makes no mistake for {team}.
Nuance: a penalty reveal replaces the sig phrasing entirely — so a penalty equaliser never says "levels it up", and a penalty winner never says "puts them ahead". The significance is carried only by the (optional) storyline sentence. Worth deciding if that trade is right.

Full-time

  • That's full-time across the board.
  • The final whistles are sounding.
  • And that's that — let's tot up the table.

Outro (wind-down after FT)

  • And that's your lot — here's how the table looks.
  • Full-time everywhere. Let's see the standings.
  • That's all she wrote — the final table.
  • The dust settles — this is how it finished.

Straight red

  • Red card at {venue}! {team} down to ten — {scorer} off.
  • {scorer} sees red for {team} at {venue}.
  • Trouble for {team} — {scorer} is dismissed at {venue}.
  • Off! {scorer} gets his marching orders at {venue}.

Second yellow

  • A second yellow at {venue} — and that's the end of {scorer}'s afternoon. {team} down to ten.
  • He's walking! A second booking for {scorer} — {team} must see this out a man light.
  • {scorer} goes for two bookable offences at {venue}. Ten men for {team}.

System

  • Team news is in — we're moments from kick-off across the National League. idle, before play
  • Replay paused — press play to resume. paused
  • Back underway — picking up the action… on resume
  • Full-time across the division. seeked to the end while paused
Nuance: "afternoon" is baked into several lines (tease "first goal of the afternoon", brace "second of the afternoon", second-yellow "end of {name}'s afternoon") — wrong for a Tuesday night game. The loader knows the KO time; an afternoon/evening switch is cheap.
